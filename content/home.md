# Polish Levels Map

A free map of Polish from the first words to fluency, in six levels from A1 to C2. Every topic is explained in plain English, with Polish examples you can listen to, short exercises and flashcards that come back when you are about to forget them. Polish has seven cases, two aspects and a lot of endings: the map shows you which ones to learn first, and why.

## How to start

- **Not sure where you are?** [Take the placement test](#/placement): 12 questions, about 6 minutes. It tells you your level and where to start on the trail.
- **New to Polish?** [Start the trail at A1](#/trail): the first stop is greetings, the alphabet and how to say who you are.
- **Just want to look around?** Open a level below. Nothing needs an account.

## How it works

1. **The trail.** Each level is a land, and each land is a path of stops: one stop per topic, with a short quiz, and a checkpoint at the end of each unit. Follow it in order, or jump to any topic from the level page.
2. **"I know this".** Tick a topic when you are confident. Your level page and the map fill in as you go, and the final test of the level checks that it holds. Every available level also has a summary and a one-page cheat sheet you can print.
3. **Review.** Wrong answers go to your mistake log, and words you save go to My words. Ready-made flashcard decks (words of each topic, case endings, core verbs) work the same way. All of them come back in short review sessions, spaced out so that each one arrives just before you would forget it. Today shows what is due.
4. **The Case Lab.** All seven cases on one map: what each one does, its endings, a "Pick the case" helper and every form of a phrase side by side.

## Your data

No accounts, no tracking: your progress is stored only in this browser. Export a backup file from the profile menu if you want to move to another device or keep a copy. Clearing your browser data deletes your progress.

## Found a mistake?

Every topic page, set result and flashcard has a quiet **Report a mistake** link. It opens a new report (an issue on GitHub) with the page already named: say what looks wrong and, if you can, what it should say. Every report is read and every confirmed mistake is fixed.
