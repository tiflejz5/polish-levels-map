# Personal pronouns in all cases

English has two forms of most pronouns: "I / me", "he / him". Polish pronouns change for every case, like nouns, and some have two or three forms. A **short** form is the normal one: *Lubię go.* (I like him.) A **long** form is for stress or contrast: *Jego lubię, a jej nie.* (Him I like, but not her.) After a preposition, *on, ona, ono, oni, one* take forms with **n-**: *z nim, dla niej, do nich*.

## How it works

| | *ja* | *ty* | *on, ono* | *ona* | *my* | *wy* | *oni, one* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| genitive (*nie ma…, dla…*) | mnie | cię / **ciebie** | go / **jego**, *niego* | jej, *niej* | nas | was | ich, *nich* |
| dative (*daję…*) | mi / **mnie** | ci / **tobie** | mu / **jemu**, *niemu* | jej, *niej* | nam | wam | im, *nim* |
| accusative (*widzę…*) | mnie | cię / **ciebie** | go / **jego**, *niego* (*ono*: je) | ją, *nią* | nas | was | ich, *nich* (*one*: je, *nie*) |
| instrumental (*z…*) | mną | tobą | nim | nią | nami | wami | nimi |
| locative (*o…*) | mnie | tobie | nim | niej | nas | was | nich |

Plain = short, the usual form. **Bold** = long, for stress and contrast. *Italic* = after a preposition.

*pan* and *pani* change like nouns: *Znam pana. Dziękuję pani. Rozmawiamy o panu.*

## When to use it

1. **Short forms by default, never first in the sentence.** *Kocham cię.* (I love you.) *Daję mu prezent.* (I'm giving him a present.)
2. **Long forms for stress or contrast.** *Mnie się podoba, a tobie?* (I like it. And you?) Also alone: *Komu? — Tobie.* (To whom? — To you.)
3. **After a preposition: the n- forms for "he, she, it, they".** *Idę do niego.* (I'm going to his place.) *Rozmawiamy o nich.* (We're talking about them.)
4. **After a preposition: the long forms for "me, you".** *To dla ciebie.* (This is for you.) *Idziesz ze mną?* (Are you coming with me?)
5. **After *nie*: the genitive, as with nouns.** *Nie znam jej.* (I don't know her.) *Nie ma go w domu.* (He isn't at home.)

## Examples

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Znasz Tomka? — Tak, znam go dobrze. | Do you know Tomek? — Yes, I know him well. |
| Zadzwonię do ciebie jutro. | I'll call you tomorrow. |
| Ania ma urodziny. Kupiłem jej kwiaty. | It's Ania's birthday. I bought her flowers. |
| Byliśmy u nich w sobotę. | We were at their place on Saturday. |
| Dziękuję wam za pomoc! | Thank you all for your help! |
| Czy mogę iść z wami? | Can I come with you? |
| Często o tobie myślę. | I often think about you. |
| Nie widzę ich. | I can't see them. |

## Good to know

- *u mnie, u ciebie, u niego* = at my, your, his place.
- *ze mną* has *ze*; *z tobą, z nim* have *z*.
- *go* is both genitive and accusative: *Widzę go* and *Nie widzę go*. *ją* (accusative) becomes *jej* after *nie*: *Widzę ją. Nie widzę jej.*
- *go, ją, je* also stand for things: *Gdzie jest mój telefon? — Mam go.* (I've got it.)

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *Kocham ty.* → ✓ *Kocham cię.* — the object needs the accusative.
- ✗ *Cię kocham.* → ✓ *Kocham cię.* — a short form never starts the sentence.
- ✗ *Idę z on.* → ✓ *Idę z nim.* — after *z* the instrumental, with *n-*.
- ✗ *To dla go.* → ✓ *To dla niego.* — after a preposition use the n- form.
- ✗ *Nie znam ją.* → ✓ *Nie znam jej.* — after *nie* the genitive.
