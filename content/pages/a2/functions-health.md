# Talk about health at the doctor's or pharmacy

English says "My head hurts" or "I have a headache". Polish turns it round: the thing that hurts is the subject and you are the object, ***Boli mnie głowa*** (literally "the head hurts me"). "I have a cold" is not *mam zimno*: say *Jestem przeziębiony / przeziębiona.* At the pharmacy you ask for something "for" an illness with *na* + accusative: *coś na kaszel*.

## Key phrases

What the doctor asks (formal)

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Co panu dolega? / Co pani dolega? | What seems to be the problem? (to a man / to a woman) |
| Od kiedy? | Since when? |

Saying what is wrong

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Boli mnie głowa. / Boli mnie gardło. | I have a headache. / I have a sore throat. |
| Bolą mnie plecy. | My back hurts. |
| Mam gorączkę. | I have a temperature. |
| Mam katar i kaszel. | I have a runny nose and a cough. |
| Jestem przeziębiona. | I have a cold. (a woman speaking) |
| Źle się czuję. | I don't feel well. |
| Od wczoraj. / Od trzech dni. | Since yesterday. / For three days. |

The doctor's advice

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Proszę brać te tabletki dwa razy dziennie. | Take these tablets twice a day. |
| Proszę dużo pić i odpoczywać. | Drink a lot and rest. |

At the pharmacy

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Czy jest coś na kaszel? | Do you have anything for a cough? |
| Poproszę coś na ból głowy. | Something for a headache, please. |
| Czy to jest bez recepty? | Is it available without a prescription? |

## Dialogue

Pani Agnieszka is at the doctor's. They use *pan / pani*.

> **Doctor:** Dzień dobry. Co pani dolega? (Good morning. What seems to be the problem?)
> **Pani Agnieszka:** Dzień dobry. Boli mnie gardło i mam katar. (Good morning. I have a sore throat and a runny nose.)
> **Doctor:** Od kiedy? (Since when?)
> **Pani Agnieszka:** Od trzech dni. Wczoraj miałam też gorączkę. (For three days. Yesterday I also had a temperature.)
> **Doctor:** Proszę otworzyć usta… Tak, gardło jest czerwone. To przeziębienie. (Please open your mouth… Yes, your throat is red. It's a cold.)
> **Pani Agnieszka:** Czy muszę brać antybiotyk? (Do I have to take an antibiotic?)
> **Doctor:** Nie, nie trzeba. Proszę dużo pić, odpoczywać i brać tabletki na gardło. (No, there's no need. Drink a lot, rest and take throat tablets.)
> **Pani Agnieszka:** Dobrze. Dziękuję, panie doktorze. (All right. Thank you, doctor.)

## Culture note

- People address a doctor as *panie doktorze* / *pani doktor*.

## Good to know

- *boleć*: *boli* for one thing, *bolą* for more: *Bolą mnie nogi.* Past: *Bolała mnie głowa.* (*głowa* is feminine.)
- The person takes the accusative: *Boli cię głowa? Boli go ząb.* ([[a2-grammar-pronouns-in-cases]])
- Words for the body and illnesses: [[a2-vocabulary-health-body-weather]].

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *Moja głowa boli.* → ✓ *Boli mnie głowa.* — Polish says "the head hurts me".
- ✗ *Boli mnie plecy.* → ✓ *Bolą mnie plecy.* — *plecy* is plural, so the verb is plural.
- ✗ *Mam zimno.* (for "I have a cold") → ✓ *Jestem przeziębiony.* — a cold is *przeziębienie*; *zimno* is the temperature.
- ✗ *Czy jest coś dla kaszlu?* → ✓ *Czy jest coś na kaszel?* — medicine "for" something is *na* + accusative.

## Practice

What do you say?

1. Say that you have a headache.
2. Say that you have a temperature.
3. Ask the pharmacist for something for a cough.
4. Say that you have had it for two days.

Choose the right word.

5. (Boli / Bolą) ___ mnie nogi.
6. Czy jest coś na (ból / bólu) ___ gardła?

Correct the mistake.

7. Moja głowa boli.

Translate into Polish.

8. I don't feel well.
