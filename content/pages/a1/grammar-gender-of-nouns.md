# The three genders of nouns

In English only people and animals have a gender (he, she), and a table is just "it". In Polish **every noun** is masculine, feminine or neuter, even a table or a cup of coffee. Gender decides the form of every adjective and pronoun next to the noun, so it is the first thing to check. The good news: the **last letter** of the dictionary form tells you the gender in most cases.

## How it works

| Gender | Usual ending | "this" | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| masculine (m) | a consonant | *ten* | *ten dom* (house), *ten telefon*, *ten kot* (cat) |
| feminine (f) | **-a** | *ta* | *ta kawa* (coffee), *ta lampa*, *ta książka* (book) |
| neuter (n) | **-o, -e, -ę, -um** | *to* | *to okno* (window), *to morze* (sea), *to imię* (name), *to muzeum* |

Exceptions worth learning now:

| Group | Gender | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| men's words ending in **-a** | masculine | *ten tata* (dad), *ten kolega* (friend), *ten mężczyzna* (man) |
| some nouns ending in a consonant | feminine | *ta noc* (night), *ta rzecz* (thing), *ta sól* (salt) |
| words ending in **-i** for women | feminine | *ta pani* (lady) |
| *dziecko* | neuter, though it is a person | *to dziecko* (child) |

Learn these with *ta*: *ta miłość* (love), *ta jesień* (autumn).

## When to use it

1. **Choosing "this":** *ten*, *ta* or *to* agrees with the noun. *Ten pokój jest duży.* (This room is big.)
2. **Choosing "he / she / it" for a thing:** a masculine thing is *on*, a feminine thing is *ona*, a neuter thing is *ono*. *Gdzie jest telefon? — On jest tu.* (Where's the phone? — It's here.)
3. **Adjectives:** the ending follows the gender: *nowy dom, nowa kawa, nowe okno*. See [[a1-cases-nominative]].
4. **Learning new words:** if the ending does not tell you, learn the noun with *ten / ta / to*: *ta noc*, *ten tata*.

## Examples

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Ten dom jest stary. | This house is old. |
| Ta kawa jest zimna. | This coffee is cold. |
| To okno jest małe. | This window is small. |
| Ten kolega jest bardzo miły. | This friend is very nice. |
| Gdzie jest lampa? — Ona jest tam. | Where's the lamp? — It's over there. |
| To muzeum jest nowe. | This museum is new. |
| Ta noc jest długa. | This night is long. |
| To dziecko jest zmęczone. | This child is tired. |

## Good to know

- *ten, ta, to* mean "this", not "the". Polish has no word for "the" or "a": *To jest dom.* (It's a house.) The *to* of *to jest* never changes: [[a1-verbs-byc]].
- In speech people often leave out the pronoun: *Gdzie jest telefon? — Jest tu.*
- Masculine nouns have smaller groups (people, animals, things). They start to matter with the accusative: [[a1-cases-accusative]].

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *Gdzie jest telefon? — Ono jest tu.* → ✓ *On jest tu.* — things are *on* or *ona* by gender; *ono* is only for neuter nouns.
- ✗ *ta tata* → ✓ *ten tata* — men's words ending in *-a* are masculine.
- ✗ *ten noc* → ✓ *ta noc* — *noc* is feminine although it ends in a consonant.
- ✗ *ta muzeum* → ✓ *to muzeum* — nouns in *-um* are neuter.
- ✗ *To jest ten dom.* (for "It's a house.") → ✓ *To jest dom.* — *ten* means "this"; Polish has no articles.
