# Alphabet, sounds and stress

Polish spelling is far more regular than English: know the letters and you can read any word aloud. There are no silent letters, and the stress almost always falls on the **second-to-last syllable**. New for English speakers: nine letters with marks (*ą, ć, ę, ł, ń, ó, ś, ź, ż*) and letter pairs that make one sound (*sz, cz, rz, ch, dz, dż*).

## How it works

32 letters (*q, v, x* only in foreign words):

*a ą b c ć d e ę f g h i j k l ł m n ń o ó p r s ś t u w y z ź ż*

Letters that surprise English speakers:

| Letter | Sounds like | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *c* | "ts" in cats | *noc* (night) |
| *ch*, *h* | "ch" in Scottish loch | *chleb* (bread), *herbata* (tea) |
| *cz* | "ch" in church | *czas* (time) |
| *sz* | "sh" in shop | *szkoła* (school) |
| ***rz***, ***ż*** | "s" in pleasure | *rzeka* (river), *żona* (wife) |
| *j* | "y" in yes | *jajko* (egg) |
| *w* | "v" in van | *woda* (water) |
| *ł* | "w" in water | *mały* (small) |
| *y* | "i" in bit | *ty* (you) |
| ***ó***, ***u*** | "oo" in book | *ołówek* (pencil), *ucho* (ear) |
| *ą* | nasal "on", as in French bon | *są* (they are) |
| *ę* | nasal "en"; at the end of a word almost a plain "e" | *ręka* (hand), *dziękuję* (thank you) |

**Soft sounds** have two spellings: a mark before a consonant or at the end of a word, an *i* before a vowel. If the vowel is *i* itself, just write it: *zima* (winter), *nic* (nothing).

| Mark | Before a vowel | Sounds like | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *ś* | *si* | a soft "sh" (tongue flat, as in sheep) | *środa*, *siostra* |
| *ć* | *ci* | a soft "ch" (as in cheese) | *cześć*, *ciocia* |
| *ź* | *zi* | a soft "zh" | *źle*, *zima* |
| *dź* | *dzi* | a soft "j" (as in jeep) | *dzień* |
| *ń* | *ni* | "ny" in canyon | *koń*, *nie* |

**Stress:** the second-to-last syllable. An *i* before a vowel is not a syllable of its own: *Ka-sia* has two.

## When to use it

1. **Reading a new word:** say every letter or letter pair; an *i* before a vowel only softens the sound before it. *Kraków* = *Kra-ków*.
2. **Stress:** count syllables from the end. *War-sza-wa* → war-**SZA**-wa (Warsaw).
3. **Marks change words:** *sad* (orchard) and *sąd* (court) are different words. *rz / ż*, *ó / u* and *ch / h* sound the same, so learn each word with its spelling.

## Examples

| Polish | English | Stress |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dzień dobry. | Good morning. / Hello. | *dzień DO-bry* |
| Dziękuję. | Thank you. | *dzię-KU-ję* |
| Przepraszam. | Excuse me. / Sorry. | *prze-PRA-szam* |
| Warszawa | Warsaw | *war-SZA-wa* |
| herbata | tea | *her-BA-ta* |
| poniedziałek | Monday | *po-nie-DZIA-łek* |

## Good to know

- At the end of a word *b, d, g, w, z* sound like *p, t, k, f, s*: *chleb* sounds like "chlep", *Kraków* like "Krakuf".
- After *p, t, k, ch* the pair *rz* sounds like *sz*: *przepraszam*, *trzy* (three).

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *Lodz* → ✓ *Łódź* — *Lodz* is the English spelling; in Polish the marks are part of the letters.
- ✗ *woda* said with an English "w" → ✓ *woda* with a "v" — Polish *w* is a v sound (an f at the end of a word).
- ✗ *WAR-sza-wa* → ✓ *war-SZA-wa* — stress the second-to-last syllable, not the first.
- ✗ *rzeka* said as "r" + "z" → ✓ *rzeka* with the "s" of *pleasure* — *rz* is one sound, the same as *ż*.

## Practice

Write the stressed syllable in capitals.

1. *te-le-fon*
2. *her-ba-ta*
3. *ro-wer*

Which spelling makes the same sound?

4. *ż* sounds the same as ___.
5. *ó* sounds the same as ___.

Add the missing marks.

6. *Dzien dobry.*
7. *Przepraszam, ktora godzina?* (Excuse me, what time is it?)

How do you say it?

8. The *w* in *woda* sounds like English ___.

## Answers

1. te-LE-fon
2. her-BA-ta
3. RO-wer
4. rz
5. u
6. Dzień dobry.
7. Przepraszam, która godzina?
8. v
