# Personal pronouns and formal address

English has one "you" for everyone. Polish has *ty* (one person) and *wy* (several people) for friends, family and children, and *pan* (to a man), *pani* (to a woman), *państwo* (to a group) for everyone else. The polite words really mean "sir", "madam" and "ladies and gentlemen", so the verb after them is the "he / she / they" form. Polish also has two words for "they", and often leaves pronouns out.

## How it works

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | *ja* (I) | *my* (we) |
| 2nd, informal | *ty* (you, one friend) | *wy* (you, several friends) |
| 3rd | *on* (he), *ona* (she), ***ono*** (it) | ***oni*** (they: a group with at least one man), ***one*** (they: all other groups) |

Formal "you":

| Talking to | Polish | Verb form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a man | *pan* | like *on*: *Jak się pan ma?* |
| a woman | *pani* | like *ona*: *Jak się pani ma?* |
| a mixed group, or a couple | ***państwo*** | like *oni*: *Jak się państwo mają?* |
| a group of men / of women | *panowie* / *panie* | like *oni* / *one* |

*ono* is for neuter nouns, for example *dziecko* (child). Things are usually *on* or *ona*: a phone (*telefon*) is *on*, a lamp (*lampa*) is *ona*: see [[a1-grammar-gender-of-nouns]].

## When to use it

1. **Usually leave the pronoun out.** The verb ending already says who: *Jak się masz?* (How are you?), not *Jak się ty masz?*
2. **Use it for contrast or emphasis.** *Ja jestem Ewa, a ty?* (I'm Ewa, and you?)
3. **Use *ty* with friends, family and children.** *Cześć, Ola! Jak się masz?* (Hi, Ola! How are you?)
4. **Use *pan / pani* with strangers, in shops, at work and with older people.** *Dzień dobry. Jak się pani ma?* (Hello. How are you, madam?)
5. **Use *oni* if the group has at least one man, *one* if it has none.** *Ewa i Marek? Oni są tu.* (Ewa and Marek? They're here.)

## Examples

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Jak się pan ma? | How are you? (to a man) |
| Dobrze, dziękuję. A pani? | Fine, thank you. And you? (to a woman) |
| Dzień dobry! Jak się państwo mają? | Hello! How are you? (formal, to a couple or a group) |
| Cześć! Jak się macie? | Hi! How are you? (to several friends) |
| Kasia? Ona jest tu. | Kasia? She's here. |
| Ania i Ola? One są tam. | Ania and Ola? They're over there. |
| Piotr i Ewa? Oni są w domu. | Piotr and Ewa? They're at home. |
| My jesteśmy z Gdańska, a wy? | We're from Gdańsk, and you? |

*jest* (is) and *są* (are) come from *być* (to be): [[a1-verbs-byc]].

## Good to know

- *Proszę pana* / *proszę pani* is how you politely get someone's attention: "Excuse me, sir / madam".
- Moving from *pan / pani* to *ty* is a social step; usually the older person offers it.

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *Jak się pan masz?* → ✓ *Jak się pan ma?* — *pan* takes the "he" form of the verb, not the "you" form.
- ✗ *Jak się masz?* to a stranger → ✓ *Jak się pan ma?* / *Jak się pani ma?* — *ty* to strangers sounds rude or too familiar.
- ✗ *Jak się macie?* to one older person → ✓ *Jak się pani ma?* — *wy* is never the polite form for one person.
- ✗ *Anna i Ewa? Oni są tu.* → ✓ *Anna i Ewa? One są tu.* — *oni* needs at least one man in the group.
- ✗ *Ja jestem Tom. Ja jestem tu.* → ✓ *Jestem Tom. Jestem tu.* — the verb shows the person; repeating *ja* sounds heavy.
