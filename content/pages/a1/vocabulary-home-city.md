# Home, furniture and places in town

Most of these words are simple nouns, but two things work differently from English. First, "in" and "at" a place are not always *w*: you are *w sklepie* (in the shop) but *na poczcie* (at the post office), so learn each place with its little word. Second, *drzwi* (door) is plural only, like English "scissors": *Drzwi są otwarte.*

## Good to know

- *w* or *na*? Say *w sklepie, w banku, w szkole, w kinie*, but *na poczcie, na dworcu, na przystanku, na rynku*. Learn them as phrases for now; the rule comes with [[a1-cases-locative-places]].
- *parter* is the ground floor, and *pierwsze piętro* is the floor above it (British "first floor", American "second floor").
- *pokój* also means "peace". *blok* is a block of flats.
- *dworzec* is a station building for trains or buses; *przystanek* is a stop in the street.

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *Jestem w poczcie.* → ✓ *Jestem na poczcie.* — some places take *na*, not *w*.
- ✗ *Czekam w przystanku.* → ✓ *Czekam na przystanku.* — most places take *w*; learn the *na*-places as a list: *poczta, dworzec, przystanek, rynek*.
- ✗ *Drzwi jest otwarte.* → ✓ *Drzwi są otwarte.* — *drzwi* is plural only.
- ✗ *Gdzie jest stacja autobusowa?* (for a bus stop in the street) → ✓ *Gdzie jest przystanek?* — a stop is *przystanek*.
- ✗ *Mam duży mieszkanie.* → ✓ *Mam duże mieszkanie.* — *mieszkanie* is neuter: *-e*.
