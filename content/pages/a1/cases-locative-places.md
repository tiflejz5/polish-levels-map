# Locative: saying where you are

In English the noun after "in" or "at" never changes: "in Kraków", "at home". In Polish the noun after *w* (in, at) and *na* (at, on) takes a special ending: *Kraków → w Krakowie*, *praca → w pracy*. This is the **locative** (*miejscownik*, questions: *(o) kim? (o) czym?*). It never stands alone, always after a preposition, and answers *Gdzie?* (Where?).

## How it works

Singular. Most nouns take **-e**, and the consonant softens.

| Gender | Ending | Nominative → locative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| masculine, neuter | **-e** (consonant softens) | *Kraków → w Krakowie*, *sklep → w sklepie*, *kino → w kinie* |
| masculine, neuter | **-u** after *k, g, ch, c* and soft consonants | *park → w parku*, *bank → w banku*, *hotel → w hotelu*, *mieszkanie → w mieszkaniu* |
| feminine | **-e** (consonant softens) | *Warszawa → w Warszawie*, *szkoła → w szkole*, *Polska → w Polsce* |
| feminine | **-i / -y** after soft consonants, *-ia, -ja, -ca* | *kuchnia → w kuchni*, *restauracja → w restauracji*, *praca → w pracy*, *ulica → na ulicy* |
| neuter in *-um* | no change | *w muzeum*, *w centrum* |

The softening before **-e**:

| Change | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| *k → c*, *g → dz* | *Polska → w Polsce*, *apteka → w aptece*, *Praga → w Pradze* |
| *t → ci*, *d → dzi* | *poczta → na poczcie*, *ogród → w ogrodzie* |
| *r → rz*, *ł → l* | *biuro → w biurze*, *szkoła → w szkole*, *stół → na stole* |
| *w → wi*, *p → pi*, *n → ni* | *Kraków → w Krakowie* (and *ó → o*), *sklep → w sklepie*, *kino → w kinie* |

Adjectives: masculine and neuter **-ym** (**-im** after *k, g*), feminine **-ej**: *w małym mieszkaniu*, *w polskim mieście*, *w nowej szkole*.

## When to use it

1. **After *w* for most places: buildings, rooms, towns and countries.** *Ola jest w kuchni.* (Ola is in the kitchen.)
2. **After *na* for a list of places:** *na poczcie, na dworcu, na przystanku, na rynku, na lotnisku, na uniwersytecie, na ulicy*. *Czekam na przystanku.* (I'm waiting at the stop.)
3. **After *na* for events and surfaces:** *na koncercie, na lekcji, na stole*.
4. **Only for "where", not "where to":** *Jestem w sklepie*, but *Idę do sklepu*. See [[a1-verbs-going-isc-jechac]].

## Examples

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Gdzie jesteś? — W domu. | Where are you? — At home. |
| Mieszkamy w małym mieszkaniu w centrum. | We live in a small flat in the centre. |
| Pani Ewa jest teraz w pracy. | Mrs Ewa is at work now. |
| Moja siostra studiuje na uniwersytecie w Poznaniu. | My sister studies at the university in Poznań. |
| Paweł czeka na dworcu. | Paweł is waiting at the station. |
| Telefon jest na stole w kuchni. | The phone is on the table in the kitchen. |

## Good to know

- *dom* takes **-u**: *w domu* (at home).
- Some nouns lose their *e*: *przystanek → na przystanku*, *dworzec → na dworcu*.
- *miasto* changes more: *w mieście* (in town).
- More uses at A2: [[a2-cases-locative-full]].

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *Mieszkam w Kraków.* → ✓ *Mieszkam w Krakowie.* — the noun changes after *w*.
- ✗ *Jestem w domie.* → ✓ *Jestem w domu.* — *dom* takes *-u*, not *-e*.
- ✗ *Mieszkam w Polska.* → ✓ *Mieszkam w Polsce.* — *-a → -e*, and *k → c*.
- ✗ *Jestem w poczcie.* → ✓ *Jestem na poczcie.* — *poczta* is one of the *na*-places.
- ✗ *Idę w sklepie.* → ✓ *Idę do sklepu.* — the locative is for where you are, not where you are going.

Put the word in brackets into the locative.
