# być (to be) and to jest / to są

*Być* is "to be". Like English "am / is / are", it is irregular, so learn its six forms by heart. Two things are different from English: the ending already says who, so *Jestem Tom* is a full sentence without "I", and "this is / these are" is *to jest / to są*, where *to* never changes, whatever you point at.

## How it works

Present tense of *być*:

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | (*ja*) ***jestem*** (I am) | (*my*) ***jesteśmy*** (we are) |
| 2nd | (*ty*) ***jesteś*** (you are) | (*wy*) ***jesteście*** (you are) |
| 3rd | (*on, ona, ono*) ***jest*** (he / she / it is) | (*oni, one*) ***są*** (they are) |
| formal | *pan / pani* ***jest*** | *państwo* ***są*** |

Pointing:

| Polish | English | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *To jest Marek.* | This is Marek. | one person or thing: *to jest* |
| *To są moje dzieci.* | These are my children. | more than one: *to są* |
| *Kto to jest?* | Who is this? | people and animals |
| *Co to jest?* | What is this? | things |

Negative: put *nie* in front: *nie jestem*, *to nie jest*.

## When to use it

1. **Saying who you are:** *Jestem Ola.* (I'm Ola.)
2. **Describing:** *Jesteś bardzo miły.* (You're very kind. — to a man; to a woman: *miła*.)
3. **Saying where someone is:** *Gdzie jesteś? — Jestem tu.* (Where are you? — I'm here.)
4. **Introducing and pointing:** *To jest pani Nowak.* (This is Mrs Nowak.) *To są moje siostry.* (These are my sisters.)
5. **Asking:** *Czy to jest twój telefon?* (Is this your phone?) — *czy* at the start turns a statement into a yes / no question.

## Examples

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Jestem Kuba, a to jest Zosia. | I'm Kuba, and this is Zosia. |
| Gdzie jesteście? — Jesteśmy tu! | Where are you? — We're here! |
| Pan jest bardzo miły. | You are very kind. (to a man) |
| Kasia i Marek są tam. | Kasia and Marek are over there. |
| Co to jest? — To jest herbata. | What's this? — It's tea. |
| To są moje dzieci, Antek i Zuzia. | These are my children, Antek and Zuzia. |
| Nie jestem zmęczona. | I'm not tired. (a woman speaking) |
| Kto to jest? — To jest mój brat. | Who's this? — It's my brother. |

## Good to know

- The adjective after *jestem* shows your gender: a man says *jestem zmęczony*, a woman *jestem zmęczona*.
- For a job or nationality (*I'm a student*, *she's a doctor*) the noun needs a new ending after a form of *być* (*jestem, jest…*): [[a1-cases-instrumental-identity]]. After *to jest* the noun does not change: *To jest student.*
- Age uses *mieć* (to have), not *być*: *Mam trzydzieści lat* (I'm thirty). See [[a1-functions-personal-details]].
- *Tu* and *tutaj* both mean "here"; *tam* is "there".

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *Oni jest tu.* → ✓ *Oni są tu.* — the plural "they are" is *są*.
- ✗ *To są mój brat.* → ✓ *To jest mój brat.* — one person: *to jest*.
- ✗ *To jest moje dzieci.* → ✓ *To są moje dzieci.* — *dzieci* is plural, so *to są*.
- ✗ *Jestem trzydzieści.* → ✓ *Mam trzydzieści lat.* — age uses *mieć*, not *być*.
- ✗ *Ja jestem Tom, ja jestem tu.* → ✓ *Jestem Tom, jestem tu.* — *jestem* already means "I am".
