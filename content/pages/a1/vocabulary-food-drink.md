# Food and drink

Food words are easy to learn but have two traps for English speakers. First, after *jeść*, *pić*, *lubić* and *poproszę* the food is the object, so feminine words change: *kawa → Piję kawę*. Second, Polish meals do not match English ones one to one: *obiad* is the main hot meal of the day, often eaten in the afternoon, so it is "lunch" or "dinner" depending on the time.

## Good to know

- *Smacznego!* is what you say before people start eating. The answer is *Dziękuję, nawzajem.* (Thank you, you too.)
- *lody*, *ziemniaki*, *pierogi* and *truskawki* are plural, so they take *są*: *Lody są zimne.*
- *makaron* is any pasta, not only macaroni: *makaron z sosem pomidorowym* (pasta with tomato sauce).
- *o siódmej* (at seven) and *bez cukru* (without sugar) are ready phrases; the rules come with [[a1-functions-time-dates]] and [[a1-cases-genitive-basics]].

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *Lubię kawa.* → ✓ *Lubię kawę.* — after *lubić* a feminine noun takes *-ę* (accusative).
- ✗ *Poproszę jedna kawa.* → ✓ *Poproszę kawę.* — Polish has no word for "a"; *jedna* means "one" (*Poproszę jedną kawę* is fine if you mean one).
- ✗ *Lody jest dobry.* → ✓ *Lody są dobre.* — *lody* is plural in Polish.
- ✗ *kanapka z ser* → ✓ *kanapka z serem* — *z* meaning "with" takes the instrumental.
- ✗ *Jem sałatę z pomidorem i serem.* (for a mixed salad) → ✓ *Jem sałatkę z pomidorem i serem.* — *sałatka* is a salad; *sałata* is lettuce.
