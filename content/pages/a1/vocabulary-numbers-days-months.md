# Numbers 0–100, days of the week, months

Polish numbers are built like English ones: *dwadzieścia* (twenty) + *dwa* (two) = *dwadzieścia dwa* (twenty-two), with no "and" and no hyphen. Two differences matter from day one: days and months are written with a **small letter**, and "on Monday" or "in July" change the end of the word: *poniedziałek → w poniedziałek*, *lipiec → w lipcu*. Learn these short phrases as they are; the rules behind them come later.

## Good to know

- Phone numbers can be read digit by digit: *sześć, dwa, zero…* Poles often read them in groups of three, which needs the hundreds (A2).
- *we* instead of *w* before *w* or *f* followed by another consonant: *we wtorek*, *we wrześniu*. It is easier to say.
- *Jeden* changes with gender (*jedna kawa*, *jedno piwo*), and *dwa* becomes *dwie* with feminine nouns (*dwie kawy*). Counting things comes in [[a1-functions-ordering-shopping]].

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *w Poniedziałek* → ✓ *w poniedziałek* — days and months take a small letter in Polish.
- ✗ *w sobota* → ✓ *w sobotę* — "on" + a day changes the ending: *-a → -ę*.
- ✗ *pięćnaście* → ✓ *piętnaście* — 15 and 19 are spelled with *t*: *piętnaście*, *dziewiętnaście*.
- ✗ *dwadzieścia i dwa* → ✓ *dwadzieścia dwa* — no "and" inside a number.
- ✗ *w lipiec* → ✓ *w lipcu* — "in" + a month changes the ending; learn the *w …* forms as phrases.
