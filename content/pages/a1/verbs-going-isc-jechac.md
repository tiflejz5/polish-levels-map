# Going places: iść and jechać

English has one verb, "go", for walking to the shop and for flying to Australia. Polish chooses the verb by how you travel: *iść* on foot, *jechać* on anything with wheels. The word for "to" changes too: you are *w sklepie* but go *do sklepu*, you are *na poczcie* but go *na pocztę*.

## How it works

The forms, from [[a1-verbs-common-irregular]]:

| Person | *iść* (go on foot) | *jechać* (go by vehicle) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *ja* | ***idę*** | ***jadę*** |
| *ty* | idziesz | jedziesz |
| *on, ona, ono, pan, pani* | idzie | jedzie |
| *my* | idziemy | jedziemy |
| *wy* | idziecie | jedziecie |
| *oni, one, państwo* | ***idą*** | ***jadą*** |

Where you are and where you go:

| *Gdzie?* (where?) | *Dokąd?* (where to?) | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *w sklepie* | *do sklepu* | (in / to) the shop |
| *w Krakowie* | *do Krakowa* | (in / to) Kraków |
| *w domu* | *do domu* | (at) home / (go) home |
| *w pracy* | *do pracy* | (at / to) work |
| *na poczcie* | *na pocztę* | (at / to) the post office |
| *na dworcu* | *na dworzec* | (at / to) the station |
| *na koncercie* | *na koncert* | (at / to) a concert |

The rule: ***w* + locative → *do* + genitive**; ***na* + locative → *na* + accusative**.

How you travel (ready phrases for now; the rule comes with [[a2-cases-instrumental-full]]): *samochodem* (by car), *autobusem* (by bus), *tramwajem* (by tram), *pociągiem* (by train), *rowerem* (by bike), *pieszo* (on foot).

## When to use it

1. ***iść*: on foot, usually a short way in town.** *Idę do apteki.* (I'm going to the chemist's.) In town *iść* is also usual for work, school or the cinema.
2. ***jechać*: by car, bus, tram, train or bike, and to another town.** *Jadę rowerem do pracy.* (I'm cycling to work.) *Jedziemy do Gdańska.* (We're going to Gdańsk.)
3. **To a person's place: *do* + genitive.** *Idę do lekarza.* (I'm going to the doctor's.) *Jadę do babci.* (I'm going to Grandma's.)
4. **To an event or for a meal: *na* + accusative.** *Idziemy na kawę.* (We're going for a coffee.) *Ola idzie na koncert.* (Ola is going to a concert.)
5. **Now or as a plan.** *Jutro jadę do Warszawy.* (Tomorrow I'm going to Warsaw.)

## Examples

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Dokąd idziesz? — Do sklepu. | Where are you going? — To the shop. |
| Jadę autobusem do centrum. | I'm taking the bus to the centre. |
| Pan Marek jedzie do Lublina pociągiem. | Mr Marek is going to Lublin by train. |
| Idziemy na obiad do restauracji. | We're going to a restaurant for lunch. |
| W sobotę Kasia jedzie na koncert do Krakowa. | On Saturday Kasia is going to a concert in Kraków. |
| Dzieci idą do szkoły. | The children are going to school. |
| Jedziesz na dworzec? — Nie, idę pieszo. | Are you taking the bus to the station? — No, I'm walking. |
| Jest późno, idę spać. | It's late, I'm going to bed. |

## Good to know

- *idę, jadę* are for one trip, now or planned. For "every day" or "often" Polish uses other verbs (*chodzę, jeżdżę*): [[a2-verbs-motion-verbs-basics]].
- Cities and most countries take *do*: *do Polski, do Anglii, do Londynu* (a few take *na*: *na Węgry*).

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *Idę do Krakowa pociągiem.* → ✓ *Jadę do Krakowa pociągiem.* — with a vehicle named, or to another town, you need *jechać*.
- ✗ *Idę do sklep.* → ✓ *Idę do sklepu.* — *do* takes the genitive.
- ✗ *Jadę do koncertu.* → ✓ *Jadę na koncert.* — events take *na* + accusative.
- ✗ *Idę w kinie.* → ✓ *Idę do kina.* — *w kinie* says where you are, not where you are going.
- ✗ *Jadę z autobusem.* → ✓ *Jadę autobusem.* — "by bus" has no preposition; *z autobusem* means "together with a bus".
