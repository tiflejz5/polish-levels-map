# Family, describing people, nationalities, jobs

English has one word for most jobs and nationalities: "a doctor", "an American". Polish usually has a **male and a female form**: *lekarz / lekarka*, *Amerykanin / Amerykanka*. Adjectives that describe people change with gender too: *wysoki brat, wysoka siostra*. Nationality nouns take a capital letter (*Polak*), but nationality adjectives do not (*polski*).

## Good to know

- *mama* and *tata* are what people say; *matka* and *ojciec* sound more formal.
- *sympatyczny* means "likeable, friendly", not "sympathetic".
- *dziewczyna* is also any "girl", and *chłopak* any "boy" or young man.
- Jobs and nationalities after *być* take the instrumental: *Jestem Polką.* See [[a1-cases-instrumental-identity]].

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *moja brat* → ✓ *mój brat* — *brat* is masculine, so "my" is *mój*.
- ✗ *Jestem polski.* → ✓ *Jestem Polakiem.* / *Jestem Polką.* — to say your nationality use the noun, with a capital letter.
- ✗ *To jest Polski film.* → ✓ *To jest polski film.* — nationality adjectives take a small letter.
- ✗ *Ona jest lekarz.* → ✓ *Ona jest lekarką.* — the job needs the instrumental: *lekarką* (*lekarzem* is also used for women).
- ✗ *dziecki* (for "children") → ✓ *dzieci* — the plural of *dziecko* is irregular: *dzieci*.

## Practice

Give the female form.

1. lekarz → ___
2. nauczyciel → ___
3. Kanadyjczyk → ___

Complete the family.

4. mąż i ___
5. brat i ___
6. syn i ___

Translate into Polish.

7. My brother is tall.
8. My mum is a teacher.

## Answers

1. lekarka
2. nauczycielka
3. Kanadyjka
4. żona
5. siostra
6. córka
7. Mój brat jest wysoki.
8. Moja mama jest nauczycielką.
