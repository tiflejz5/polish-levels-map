# Nominative: naming things; adjective agreement

Polish nouns and adjectives change their endings to show their job in the sentence. These forms are called **cases**. The **nominative** (*mianownik*, questions: *kto? co?*) is the dictionary form: the form you name things with. Unlike English adjectives (one form, "new"), a Polish adjective agrees with the gender of its noun: *nowy dom, nowa praca, nowe auto*.

## How it works

Singular. The plural comes at A2.

| Gender | Adjective ending | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | **-y** (after *k, g*: **-i**) | *nowy dom*, *wysoki brat*, *drogi telefon* |
| feminine | **-a** | *nowa praca*, *wysoka siostra*, *droga kawa* |
| neuter | **-e** (after *k, g*: **-ie**) | *nowe auto*, *wysokie okno*, *drogie mieszkanie* |

*k* and *g* cannot be followed by *y* in Polish, so they take *i*: ***wysoki***, ***wysokie*** (never *wysoky*).

Two questions ask for the nominative:

| Question | About | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Kto to jest?* | people and animals | *To jest Marta.* *To jest kot.* |
| *Co to jest?* | things | *To jest herbata.* *To jest nowy telefon.* |

## When to use it

1. **The subject of a sentence:** *Kawa jest gorąca.* (The coffee is hot.)
2. **After *to jest / to są*:** *To jest mój nowy kolega.* (This is my new friend.)
3. **An adjective after *być*:** *Dom jest stary.* (The house is old.) *Anna jest wysoka.* (Anna is tall.)
4. **Naming and listing:** dictionaries, menus and signs: *kawa, herbata, sok*.

A noun alone after *jestem / jest* is different: *Jestem studentem.* See [[a1-cases-instrumental-identity]].

## Examples

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Co to jest? — To jest polska kawa. | What's this? — It's Polish coffee. |
| Kto to jest? — To jest pan Kowalczyk. | Who's this? — It's Mr Kowalczyk. |
| Mieszkanie jest małe, ale ładne. | The flat is small but nice. |
| Mój brat jest wysoki. | My brother is tall. |
| To jest bardzo dobra książka. | This is a very good book. |
| Gdzie jest nowy telefon? | Where's the new phone? |
| Ta herbata jest zimna! | This tea is cold! |
| To dziecko jest bardzo miłe. | This child is very nice. |

## Good to know

- The adjective usually comes before the noun: *dobra kawa*. In fixed names it comes after: *język polski* (the Polish language), *kuchnia włoska* (Italian cuisine).
- *mój, moja, moje* (my) agree the same way: *mój dom, moja kawa, moje okno*.
- A man's word ending in *-a* takes masculine adjectives: *dobry tata*, *miły kolega*.
- Adjectives of nationality take a small letter: *polska kawa*.

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *nowa dom* → ✓ *nowy dom* — *dom* is masculine, so the adjective ends in *-y*.
- ✗ *dobry kawa* → ✓ *dobra kawa* — a feminine noun needs a feminine adjective.
- ✗ *nowy auto* → ✓ *nowe auto* — *auto* ends in *-o*, so it is neuter: *-e*.
- ✗ *wysoky* → ✓ *wysoki* — after *k* and *g* write *i*, not *y*.
- ✗ *Kto to jest?* (pointing at a lamp) → ✓ *Co to jest?* — *kto* is only for people and animals.

Put the adjective in the right form.

1. (nowy) ___ praca
2. (stary) ___ okno
3. (wysoki) ___ siostra

5. (dobry) ___ tata

*Kto* or *co*?

6. ___ to jest? — To jest pani Nowak.
