# Greet people, say goodbye and introduce yourself

English uses "hello" with almost everyone. Polish makes a clear choice: *Dzień dobry* (formal) for strangers, shops, offices and older people, *Cześć* (informal) for friends, family and children. Choosing the wrong one is the first thing people notice, so learn the pairs together. To say your name, Polish does not say "my name is": it says *Mam na imię Anna* ("I have the name Anna") or simply *Jestem Anna*.

## Key phrases

Saying hello

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Dzień dobry. | Good morning. / Good afternoon. / Hello. (formal, until evening) |
| Dobry wieczór. | Good evening. (formal) |
| Cześć! | Hi! (informal) |
| Hej! | Hey! (informal) |

Saying who you are

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Mam na imię Anna. | My name is Anna. (first name) |
| Nazywam się Anna Nowak. | My name is Anna Nowak. (full name, formal) |
| Jestem Tom. | I'm Tom. |
| Jak masz na imię? | What's your name? (informal) |
| Jak się pan nazywa? / Jak się pani nazywa? | What's your name? (formal, to a man / to a woman) |
| Miło mi. / Bardzo mi miło. | Nice to meet you. |

Asking how someone is

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Jak się masz? | How are you? (informal) |
| Jak się pan ma? / Jak się pani ma? | How are you? (formal, to a man / to a woman) |
| Dobrze, dziękuję. A ty? | Fine, thanks. And you? (informal) |
| Dobrze, dziękuję. A pan? / A pani? | Fine, thank you. And you? (formal) |
| W porządku. | All right. / OK. |

Saying goodbye

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Do widzenia. | Goodbye. (formal) |
| Cześć! / Na razie! | Bye! / See you! (informal) |
| Do zobaczenia. | See you. (neutral, also formal) |
| Do jutra! | See you tomorrow! |
| Dobranoc. | Good night. (late, when you leave or go to bed) |

*Cześć* works both ways: hello and goodbye.

## Dialogue

Anna meets her neighbour, pan Adam, outside their block of flats. Her friend Piotr arrives.

> **Anna:** Dzień dobry! (Hello!)
> **Pan Adam:** Dzień dobry! Jak się pani ma? (Hello! How are you?)
> **Anna:** Dobrze, dziękuję. A pan? (Fine, thank you. And you?)
> **Pan Adam:** W porządku, dziękuję. (All right, thank you.)
> **Piotr:** Cześć, Ania! (Hi, Ania!)
> **Anna:** Cześć, Piotrek! To jest pan Adam Nowak. (Hi, Piotrek! This is Mr Adam Nowak.)
> **Piotr:** Dzień dobry. Nazywam się Piotr Wójcik. Miło mi. (Hello. My name is Piotr Wójcik. Nice to meet you.)
> **Pan Adam:** Bardzo mi miło. Do widzenia! (Very nice to meet you. Goodbye!)
> **Anna:** Do widzenia! (Goodbye!)

Anna and Piotr are friends, so they say *Cześć*. With pan Adam both use *Dzień dobry* and *Do widzenia*.

## Culture note

- Say *Dzień dobry* when you walk into a small shop, a doctor's waiting room or a lift, and *Do widzenia* when you leave.
- Neighbours and colleagues often use *pan* or *pani* with a first name: *pan Adam*, *pani Ewa*. It is polite but friendly.
- Friends use short forms of names: *Anna → Ania, Anka*; *Piotr → Piotrek*; *Katarzyna → Kasia*.
- People usually shake hands when they meet for the first time.

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *Dobry dzień!* → ✓ *Dzień dobry!* — a fixed phrase; the word order is not the English "good day".
- ✗ *Moje imię jest Tom.* → ✓ *Mam na imię Tom.* — Polish says "I have the name", or just *Jestem Tom*.
- ✗ *Jak się jesteś?* → ✓ *Jak się masz?* — the phrase uses *masz* ("have"), not "are".
- ✗ *Cześć!* to a shop assistant → ✓ *Dzień dobry!* — *cześć* is for friends, family and people your age in informal settings.
- ✗ *Dziekuje.* → ✓ *Dziękuję.* — the marks are part of the spelling.

## Practice

Formal or informal? Choose the right greeting.

1. You meet your friend Kasia: *Dzień dobry* or *Cześć*?
2. You walk into a bakery: *Dzień dobry* or *Cześć*?
3. You leave a doctor's office: *Do widzenia* or *Na razie*?

Complete the phrase.

4. Mam ___ imię Marek. (My name is Marek.)
5. Bardzo mi ___. (Very nice to meet you.)

Translate into Polish.

6. How are you? (to a friend)
7. How are you? (formal, to a woman)
8. Good evening. (formal)

## Answers

1. Cześć
2. Dzień dobry
3. Do widzenia
4. na
5. miło
6. Jak się masz?
7. Jak się pani ma?
8. Dobry wieczór.
