# Possessives and demonstratives

English "my" never changes. Polish *mój* changes with the **gender of the thing you have**, not with the owner: a man and a woman both say *mój brat* and *moja siostra*. *jego* (his), *jej* (her) and *ich* (their) are easier: they never change. "This" and "that" work like *mój*: *ten dom, ta kawa, to okno*.

## How it works

Possessives:

| English | masculine | feminine | neuter, plural (things) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| my | *mój* | *moja* | *moje* |
| your (*ty*) | *twój* | *twoja* | *twoje* |
| our | *nasz* | *nasza* | *nasze* |
| your (*wy*) | *wasz* | *wasza* | *wasze* |

***jego*** (his, its), ***jej*** (her), ***ich*** (their) and formal ***pana / pani / państwa*** (your) **never change**: *jego brat, jego siostra, jego dziecko*.

Demonstratives:

| English | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural (things) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| this | *ten* | *ta* | *to* | *te* |
| that (over there) | *tamten* | *tamta* | *tamto* | *tamte* |

As objects (accusative, see [[a1-cases-accusative]]) they change like adjectives, but only with male people and animals and with feminine nouns:

| Nominative | Accusative | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *mój brat, ten pan* | *mojego brata, tego pana* | *Znasz mojego brata?* |
| *mój telefon, ten dom* | *mój telefon, ten dom* (no change) | *Masz mój telefon?* |
| *moja siostra, ta książka* | *moją siostrę, tę książkę* | *Czytam tę książkę.* |
| *moje okno, to auto* | *moje okno, to auto* (no change) | *Lubię to miasto.* |

*twój, nasz, wasz* follow *mój*: *twojego, naszą, waszego*.

## When to use it

1. ***mój, twój, nasz, wasz* agree with the noun.** Tomek says *moja mama*; Ola says *mój tata*. (my mum, my dad)
2. ***jego, jej, ich* depend on the owner and never change.** *Kuba i jego siostra. Ola i jej brat.* (Kuba and his sister. Ola and her brother.)
3. **Formal "your" is *pana* (to a man), *pani* (to a woman), *państwa* (to a group).** *Czy to pana klucz?* (Is this your key?)
4. ***ten* for something here, *tamten* for something further away.** *Ten dom jest nowy, a tamten jest stary.* (This house is new, and that one is old.)
5. **In *To jest…* the word *to* never changes.** *To jest moja siostra.* (This is my sister.) But: *Ta pani jest z Polski.* (This lady is from Poland.)

## Examples

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| To jest mój brat, a to jest jego żona. | This is my brother, and this is his wife. |
| Twoja kawa jest na stole. | Your coffee is on the table. |
| Nasze mieszkanie jest małe, ale ładne. | Our flat is small but nice. |
| Czy to pani torba? | Is this your bag? |
| Ta książka jest ciekawa, a tamta nie. | This book is interesting, and that one isn't. |
| Znasz tego pana? — Tak, to nasz sąsiad. | Do you know this man? — Yes, he's our neighbour. |
| Lubię twoją siostrę. | I like your sister. |

## Good to know

- In speech you will often hear *tą książkę*; the standard written form is *tę książkę*.
- For a group of men the plural is different (*moi bracia*, *ci panowie*): [[a2-cases-nominative-plural]].
- *ich* is "their": *Ania i Paweł mają psa. Ich pies to Burek.*

## Common mistakes for English speakers

- ✗ *moja tata* → ✓ *mój tata* — *tata* is masculine, so *mój*; the *-a* at the end does not make it feminine.
- ✗ *nasza dziecko* → ✓ *nasze dziecko* — *dziecko* is neuter: *-e*.
- ✗ *Znam twój brat.* → ✓ *Znam twojego brata.* — as an object, a male person takes *-ego* and *-a*.
- ✗ *Ten jest mój dom.* → ✓ *To jest mój dom.* — "this is…" is always *to jest*.
- ✗ *Czy to twój telefon?* (to a stranger) → ✓ *Czy to pana telefon?* — use *pana / pani* with people you don't know.
