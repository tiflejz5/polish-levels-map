## A1 summary

**What you can do at this level**

You can greet people the right way (*Dzień dobry* or *Cześć*), say who you are, where you are from, where you live and how old you are, and ask others the same. You can order in a café, ask about prices, tell the time, and say where you are and where you are going. You speak in short sentences, and people understand you when they speak slowly and clearly. That is exactly what A1 is for.

**Key ideas**

- **Endings do the work of English word order.** *Lubię kawę*, not *Lubię kawa*: the object has its own ending. Five cases already appear at A1: nominative, instrumental, accusative, genitive and locative.
- **Every noun has a gender, and the last letter usually tells you which.** Adjectives, *mój* and *ten* follow it: *mój nowy dom*, *moja nowa praca*, *moje nowe auto*.
- **The verb ending says who.** *Mieszkam w Krakowie* is a full sentence; leave out *ja* unless you want to stress it.
- **Formal "you" is *pan* or *pani* with the "he / she" form:** *Jak się pani ma?* Use *ty* only with friends, family and children.
- **Learn chunks before rules.** *w domu*, *do pracy*, *z Polski*, *o ósmej*, *Mam … lat* work long before you know why.

**The most common trap**

Leaving every noun in its dictionary form, as English does: *Mam brat*, *Nie mam czas*, *Mieszkam w Kraków*. Check the little word or verb in front of the noun (*mam*, *nie*, *w*, *do*, *z*) and choose the ending it asks for: *Mam brata. Nie mam czasu. Mieszkam w Krakowie.*

**How long it takes**

- Reaching A1 from zero: typically **about 100–150 hours** of guided study, plus your own practice.
- Polish usually takes English speakers longer than a Romance or Germanic language such as Spanish or German: the US Foreign Service Institute puts Polish in its group of harder languages for English speakers. Much of the extra effort goes into the endings.
- At 3–4 hours a week, that is roughly **6–12 months** for the level.

*The numbers are a typical range, not a promise: your pace depends on how often you study, how much you practise on your own and how much Polish you hear every day.*

**What next**

You are ready for A2 when you can talk for two or three minutes about yourself, your family and your day, ask a few questions back, and order and pay without switching to English. A2 adds the full past tense and the future, plural endings in the cases, the dative, and the first pairs of verbs for finished and unfinished actions.
