## A2 summary

**What you can do at this level**

You can tell a short story about last weekend or a holiday and say what you are going to do next. You can buy a train ticket, book a table or a room, ask the way and follow simple directions, try on clothes and ask for another size, and explain at the doctor's or the pharmacy what hurts. You can suggest a plan, accept it or say no politely with a reason. You still speak in short, simple sentences, but you get through everyday situations in Polish.

**Key ideas**

- **Verbs come in pairs.** The imperfective is for a process or a habit (*czytałam*, *codziennie kupowałam*), the perfective for one finished result (*przeczytałam*, *kupiłam*). Learn every new verb as a pair: *robić / zrobić*.
- **Two futures, chosen by aspect.** *Będę pracować* for an activity over time, *zrobię* for one thing that gets done. *Będę* never goes with a perfective verb.
- **The plural splits into men and the rest.** A group with at least one man takes the masculine personal forms: *byli, studenci, tych studentów*. Women, children, animals and things take the simpler ones: *były, koty, te książki*.
- **Numbers change the noun.** *jeden kot*, *dwa koty*, *pięć kotów*; after *dużo, kilka, ile?* things you count take the genitive plural: *dużo ludzi, kilka minut* (but *dużo czasu*).
- **Six of the seven cases are now in use** (the vocative comes at B1). The dative joins them for the person who receives or feels something: *Daję mamie prezent. Podoba mi się. Jest mi zimno.*

**The most common trap**

Using one verb for every kind of action, as English uses "go" or "buy". Polish asks two questions. Is it one finished result or a process or habit (*kupiłam* or *kupowałam*)? Is it one trip now or a regular one (*idę* or *chodzę*)? *Codziennie chodzę do pracy*, not *Codziennie idę do pracy*; *Jutro zrobię zakupy*, not *Jutro będę zrobić zakupy*.

**How long it takes**

- Going from A1 to A2: typically **about another 100–150 hours** of guided study, plus your own practice, so roughly 200–300 hours from zero.
- Polish usually takes English speakers longer than a Romance or Germanic language such as Spanish or German: the US Foreign Service Institute puts Polish in its group of harder languages for English speakers. At A2 much of the extra effort goes into aspect and the plural endings.
- At 3–4 hours a week, that is roughly **6–12 months** for the level.

*The numbers are a typical range, not a promise: your pace depends on how often you study, how much you practise on your own and how much Polish you hear every day.*

**What next**

You are ready for B1 when you can talk for a few minutes about something that happened to you, with the right verb of each pair, and handle a ticket office, a shop or a doctor's visit without switching to English. B1 turns the *chciałbym* chunk into the full conditional and adds the whole system of prefixed verbs of motion (*wyjść, wejść, przyjechać*), reported speech, the vocative, and counting people (*dwóch studentów*).
